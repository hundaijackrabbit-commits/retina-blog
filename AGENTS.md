# Retina.blog — Product Rules

## Product identity
Retina.blog is a premium digital publication about retinal imaging, OCT/OCTA, retinal AI, research, clinical advances, emerging technology, industry, and oculomics.

It is NOT a clinic website, optometry store, generic medical blog, SaaS landing page, or AI content farm.

Target feel: scientific journal × premium technology publication × modern editorial magazine.

## Locked brand
- Brand: RETINA.BLOG
- Hero: **See What's Next.**
- Positioning: **The technologies, research and ideas shaping the future of retinal care.**
- Editorial rule: **Evidence first. Interpretation second. Hype last.**

Do not change these without explicit instruction.

## Voice
Authoritative, sophisticated, calm, precise, curious, accessible, evidence-conscious.
Avoid hype, clickbait, generic startup copy, exaggerated AI claims, and medical sensationalism.

Preferred editorial logic:
1. What changed?
2. What does the evidence say?
3. Why does it matter?
4. What are the limitations?
5. What's next?

## Visual direction
Use strong editorial typography, generous whitespace, thin rules, disciplined grids, strong image crops, near-black text, light neutral backgrounds, and restrained retina-inspired red/orange accents.

Avoid generic hospital blue, excessive rounded SaaS cards, glassmorphism, neon, giant gradients, stock doctors smiling at camera, cartoon medical graphics, clutter, and gimmicky animation.

## Image rules
Real fundus/retina imagery: hero, flagship stories, topic hubs.
OCT/OCTA: imaging, innovation, explainers, Research / Decoded.
Research/lab photography: research, AI, gene therapy, biomarkers.
Human/clinical photography: sparingly.
External-eye/iris photography: atmosphere only; never imply it is retinal imaging.
Scientific figures: explain concepts and evidence.

Never mislabel pathology as normal anatomy.
Never assume an image is ocular just because it says OCT.

DO NOT USE if it appears in the repo:
`Optical_coherence_tomography_conducted_using_the_Ultreon_1.0_Software.png`
It is coronary/intravascular OCT, not retinal OCT.

## Article UX to preserve/improve
Support category, headline, deck, hero media, author/reviewer, dates, reading time, evidence status, key takeaway, article content, figures, limitations, What's Next, references, and related reading.

## SEO / evidence rules
SEO should guide structure without keyword stuffing.
Preserve semantic headings, descriptive URLs, internal linking, topic hubs, metadata, sitemap, robots, author identity, and image alt text.
Do not fabricate search volume or produce thin pages.

Future evidence model must remain compatible with fields such as DOI, PMID, study type, evidence level, peer-review status, publication, limitations, date evidence reviewed, article author, and reviewer.

## Patch discipline
Before editing: inspect repo, inspect styles/components, inspect /public/images, run current build, then plan.
Prefer incremental improvements. Do not rewrite unrelated working architecture.
Do not add major dependencies unless necessary.

## Forbidden in Patch 1
Do not connect a CMS, database, auth, payments, ads, newsletter backend, or new framework.
Do not generate dozens of filler articles.
Do not replace the information architecture.
Do not replace the project with a template.
Do not remove evidence/editorial standards.
Do not change the brand copy above.

## Performance / accessibility
Use responsive image optimization where appropriate. Avoid layout shift and unnecessary JS. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Maintain semantic HTML, visible focus states, keyboard navigation, good contrast, descriptive alt text, and no horizontal overflow.

## Definition of success
Retina.blog should feel credible enough for researchers, interesting enough for technologists, clear enough for sophisticated general readers, and visually distinctive enough to remember.
