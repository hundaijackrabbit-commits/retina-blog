# Retina.blog Imaging Research Pass — August 2026

## Scope

This research file supports the dedicated Imaging content pass. It covers OCT, OCTA, swept-source versus spectral-domain OCT, fluorescein angiography, imaging artifacts, ultra-widefield imaging, and adaptive optics.

Editorial rule for this pass:

> The image is evidence, not the whole answer.

Each article distinguishes the physical signal being measured from the processed image shown to the reader. Claims were written as original synthesis from source facts; no source paragraph was copied or sentence-spun.

## Article map

### What Is OCT? A Modern Guide to Optical Coherence Tomography
Slug: `oct-explained`

Primary evidence:
- Huang D, et al. *Optical coherence tomography.* Science. 1991. PMID 1957169. DOI 10.1126/science.1957169.
- Comparison of retinal thicknesses measured using swept-source and spectral-domain OCT devices. PMID 25707041.
- Comparison of choroidal thickness measurements between SD-OCT and SS-OCT in normal and diseased eyes. PMID 27881909.

Claim boundaries:
- OCT is described as low-coherence interferometric, depth-resolved structural imaging.
- Device-specific thickness values are not described as universally interchangeable.
- The article does not provide disease-specific diagnostic advice.

### OCT vs. OCTA: What’s the Difference?
Slug: `oct-vs-octa`

Primary evidence:
- Jia/Yali Huang group SSADA methods paper. PMID 22418228.
- Inoue M, et al. Type 1 neovascularization comparison of OCTA and fluorescein angiography. PMID 27409488.
- Lauermann JL, et al. Segmentation and motion artifacts across retinal diseases. PMID 29982897.

Claim boundaries:
- Structural OCT is framed primarily as anatomy; OCTA as motion-derived flow contrast.
- OCTA is not described as directly measuring leakage.

### OCT Angiography Explained
Slug: `octa-explained`

Primary evidence:
- Split-spectrum amplitude-decorrelation angiography. PMID 22418228.
- Segmentation and motion artifact prevalence. PMID 29982897.
- Clinical features related to OCTA artifacts in diabetic macular edema. PMID 38447922.

Claim boundaries:
- “No detected flow” is not equated with “no blood flow.”
- Quantitative OCTA metrics are described as device- and processing-dependent.

### Swept-Source vs. Spectral-Domain OCT
Slug: `swept-source-vs-spectral-domain-oct`

Primary evidence:
- Retinal thickness comparison across SS-OCT and SD-OCT devices. PMID 25707041.
- Prospective choroidal-thickness comparison. PMID 27881909.

Claim boundaries:
- Swept-source is not described as universally superior.
- Cross-device measurement consistency is emphasized.

### Fluorescein Angiography vs. OCTA
Slug: `fluorescein-angiography-vs-octa`

Primary evidence:
- Inoue M, et al. OCTA + structural OCT versus FA for type 1 neovascularization. PMID 27409488.
- Oliveira T, et al. OCTA compared with FA, ICGA and OCT in pigment epithelial detachment. PMID 31012539.
- OCTA versus FA in chronic central serous chorioretinopathy. PMID 31238419.

Claim boundaries:
- FA is described as providing dynamic dye transit/leakage information.
- OCTA is described as motion-contrast vascular imaging.
- No modality is presented as a universal replacement for the other.

### Retinal Imaging Artifacts
Slug: `retinal-imaging-artifacts`

Primary evidence:
- Lauermann JL, et al. PMID 29982897.
- Clinical Features Related to OCT Angiography Artifacts in DME. PMID 38447922.
- OCT Angiography Artifacts in Glaucoma. PMID 33819524.

Claim boundaries:
- Artifact frequencies are tied to the cited study populations and not generalized as universal rates.
- The article distinguishes raw anatomy from downstream quantitative outputs.

### Ultra-Widefield Retinal Imaging
Slug: `ultra-widefield-retinal-imaging`

Primary evidence:
- DRCR Retina Network prospective multicenter longitudinal study. PMID 35980608. DOI 10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2022.3131.
- Prospective comparison of UWF and two-field fundus imaging. PMID 41792656.
- Peripheral hemorrhage density and DR progression, 2026. PMID 42120190. DOI 10.1136/bjo-2025-327940.

Important nuance:
- In the DRCR study, color-defined predominantly peripheral lesions were not independently associated with 4-year worsening, while FA-defined PPL were. The article preserves that distinction rather than repeating a broad “peripheral lesions predict progression” claim.

### Adaptive Optics Retinal Imaging
Slug: `adaptive-optics-retinal-imaging`

Primary evidence:
- Roorda A, et al. *Adaptive optics scanning laser ophthalmoscopy.* 2002. PMID 19436374.
- Macular cone abnormalities in retinitis pigmentosa with preserved central vision. PMID 24260224.
- Assessing photoreceptor structure in RP and Usher syndrome. PMID 27145477.
- KEYS study: two-year prospective natural history of EYS-associated RP using adaptive optics. PMID 42252293.

Claim boundaries:
- AO is described as a research and biomarker platform, not as established routine clinical diagnosis.
- Cellular visibility is not equated with a validated clinical endpoint.

## Originality / duplication controls

This pass intentionally replaces the earlier shallow versions of:
- `oct-explained`
- `oct-vs-octa`
- `ultra-widefield`
- `adaptive-optics-retinal-imaging`

The old `ultra-widefield` slug is retired and replaced by `ultra-widefield-retinal-imaging`.

The new pieces are separated by search intent:
- OCT guide = foundational structural imaging
- OCT vs OCTA = modality comparison
- OCTA explained = mechanism and interpretation
- SS vs SD OCT = hardware architecture comparison
- FA vs OCTA = vascular modality comparison
- artifacts = quality control and failure modes
- UWF = peripheral field of view
- adaptive optics = cellular-resolution frontier

No article should be generated by paraphrasing a single paper. Source details are used as factual anchors; the explanatory narrative is original Retina.blog synthesis.

## Editorial QA before publication

- Verify every PMID and DOI resolves.
- Keep “cleared,” “approved,” “diagnostic,” and “screening” terminology precise when later linking these imaging pieces to AI/industry coverage.
- Do not label a dark OCTA region as nonperfusion without context.
- Do not claim OCTA directly demonstrates leakage.
- Do not claim UWF imaging replaces dilated peripheral examination.
- Do not claim AO cellular biomarkers are established clinical endpoints.
- Preserve source dates and evidence-review date when updating.
